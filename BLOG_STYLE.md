# MUURO Blog — Writing Rules

The rulebook for every article on the MUURO journal. Applies to new
articles and to revisions of existing ones (including
`content/blog/the-paper-that-outlives-you.mdx`). When in doubt, this
file wins.

## 1. Language and tone

- **English.** International market, no localisation at launch.
- **Write like a real person who makes the product.** Not a marketer,
  not an encyclopaedia, not an AI.
- **First person, studio voice.** "We print on", "we chose this paper",
  "we kept thinking about". MUURO is "we".
- **Direct and calm.** State, do not persuade. No "amazing",
  "stunning", "elevate your space", "must-have".
- **Dry humour and personal opinion are allowed.** A Berlin studio, not
  a corporation.

## 2. Hard bans

- **No dashes.** Not the em dash, not the en dash. Only full stops,
  commas, parentheses, colons. This is law, no exceptions.
- **No AI patterns:**
  - never open with "In today's world", "In an age of", "Whether you're"
  - no "It's not just X, it's Y"
  - no "Let's dive in", "Let's explore"
  - no triplets of adjectives for rhythm ("sleek, modern, timeless")
  - no "From X to Y, we've got you covered"
  - no filler rhetorical questions opening a paragraph
  - no "Imagine a world where"
  - no closer in the style "So, the next time you..."
- **No emoji** in the body.
- **No exclamation marks.**
- **Do not lie.** If we do not know a number, we do not invent one. If
  it is an estimate, we say it is an estimate.

## 3. Length

- Depends on article type (see section 5), baseline:
  - Short (Editions story): 600 to 900 words
  - Medium (Walls, practical): 900 to 1400 words
  - Long (Materials, technical longread): 1500 to 2200 words
- **Shorter and denser beats longer and watery.** Every paragraph
  carries a fact or a thought.

## 4. Article structure

- **Headline (H1):** specific, no clickbait. "The paper that outlives
  you", not "10 reasons why paper matters".
- **Deck (subhead):** one or two sentences, set the stakes. Why read
  this.
- **Body:** H2 subheads every 200 to 400 words. H3 inside where needed.
- **One pull quote** per article. The line that later goes to
  Instagram. The strongest thought in the text.
- **One Product CTA** near the end. Links to one specific edition, not
  "the whole catalogue".
- **Conclusion:** short, no "in conclusion". Closes the thought, does
  not summarise the article.

## 5. Three categories (each article sits in exactly one)

- **Editions** — stories of specific works. Why 63 Sting Ray, what The
  Outlier means. Sell through storytelling. Warmest click-through to
  product.
- **Materials** — paper, frames, light, printing. Technical longreads.
  Build expertise, attract the discerning reader, earn links.
- **Walls** — interior scenarios. How to hang, what size above a sofa,
  paired prints. Largest traffic volume, coldest audience.

## 6. SEO requirements (never at the cost of the text)

- Each article targets **one primary keyword** plus 2 to 3 secondary.
- Keyword in the headline, in the first paragraph, in one H2,
  naturally. No stuffing.
- **Title tag** under 60 characters, **meta description** under 160.
- 2 to 3 internal links to other pages (editions, manifesto, other
  articles).
- Text is for humans, optimisation is secondary. If the choice is
  between "reads alive" and "better for SEO", choose alive.

## 7. Images

- One hero at the top.
- Inline images only when they earn it (macro of the paper, frame
  corner, process). No decorative stock.
- While there is no real photography, a brand-palette SVG placeholder.

## 8. Repurposing (build it in while writing)

Every article is written so it yields:
- **Newsletter** (lead plus 2 paragraphs plus a link)
- **IG carousel** (from the subheads plus the pull quote)
- **IG reel** (30 seconds from the intro)

So the pull quote and the subheads must work when pulled out of
context.

## 9. Byline and meta

- **Author:** always `MUURO`. No personal names.
- **Date:** month and year.
- **Reading time:** calculated automatically.

## 10. What we do not do

- No articles "for the sake of content" without a topic and keyword.
- No repeating what another article already said.
- No thin 300-word articles (they hurt the whole domain's SEO).
- No more than one product CTA (it scatters attention).
- No writing what we are not factually sure of.
